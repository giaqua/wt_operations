Irrigation-Class A Unristricted

Agricultural Use Irrigation of all types of crops — especially food crops consumed raw — using any irrigation method is permitted. Urban Use Irrigation of public gardens, golf courses, playgrounds, and other areas frequented by the public is allowed, provided that the public does not have access to these areas during irrigation. Use is also permitted for street and walkway cleaning, as well as for firefighting water. Industrial Use For industrial applications with no restrictions on public access, including aerosol-related uses.

Irrigation-Class B Ristricted 

Agricultural Use Irrigation of all types of crops is permitted, except for plants whose edible parts come into direct contact with the treated water, unless such crops are further processed or treated. Irrigation is allowed for processed food crops, food crops consumed raw using localized (non-contact) irrigation methods, and for pastures. A minimum setback distance of 60 meters from public access areas must be maintained when sprinkler irrigation is used. Urban Use Irrigation of green belts, road medians, and areas not frequented by the public is permitted. Industrial Use Industrial applications are allowed under restricted-access conditions, excluding aerosol-generating uses.

Note:		
		
a. The sanitary sewer network should not receive wastewater containing more than 60% (by volume or organic load) of industrial wastewater without obtaining prior approval from the competent authority for discharge into the sanitary network.		
		
b. It is prohibited to discharge wastewater into the sanitary network at a temperature higher than 60°C or containing concentrations exceeding the specified limits, or any matter that may damage the network materials or affect its performance. The pH value should range between 5 and 10. Discharging heavy oils, greases, wax, tar, or any solid or viscous substances that could obstruct the flow or reduce the capacity of the network is strictly prohibited. Industrial facilities must ensure that the quality of discharged water meets the approved limits before connecting to the sewer network.		
		
c. Oil interceptors (grease traps) must be installed for facilities such as oil and grease workshops, car wash stations, restaurants, and laundries. This is a general requirement for all facilities discharging oily wastewater, and they must obtain an official permit to ensure compliance with the applicable standards.		
		
		
Prohibited Substances		
		
The discharge of the following liquid wastes into the municipal sewer network is strictly prohibited:		
		
1. Industrial oils or flammable liquids, including solvents, fuels, gasoline, petroleum residues, kerosene, turpentine, or similar substances.		
		
2. Industrial solid wastes such as sand, glass, metals, or other similar materials.		
		
3. Toxic substances such as cyanides, sulfides, heavy metals, pesticides, or herbicides.		
		
4. Acids or alkalis that can cause corrosion or damage to pipes.		
		
5. Infectious waste from hospitals, healthcare facilities, research centers, or laboratories.		
		
6. Radioactive materials from treatment plants or scientific research stations.		
		
7. Explosive or flammable materials such as gasoline, benzene, naphtha, or other related compounds.		
		
8. Stormwater or roof drainage unless approved by the competent authority.		
		
9. Animal or poultry slaughter waste, or any similar organic material.		
		
10. Waste containing human or animal pathogens, or materials that may endanger human health.		
		
11. Highly odorous or colored wastes, or any substance that adversely affects the treatment process.
